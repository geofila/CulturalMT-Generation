05 / MEASUREMENT & METHODOLOGY

The detail behind the numbers.

Defined metrics, clear denominators and downloadable aggregate results.

01The reviewed collection+

The CulturalMT human-review study contains 341 Greek-to-English cultural heritage descriptions, each assessed and post-edited by one of five reviewers. Source descriptions contain 588–600 characters. The annotations were completed in May–June 2026.

Overall judgements and highlight statistics use all 341 completed annotations. Post-editing metrics use 339 pairs with a single distinct submitted version. Two records with different submitted versions are excluded from these calculations; one identical duplicate submission is counted once. Cancelled annotations and drafts are excluded.

The English reviewer submissions are post-edits of the generated descriptions. The original-source consultation figure records the reviewer’s response to the study’s source-needed question.

Explore the reference dataset ↗
02Word minimality & edit distance+

Word minimality (97.25%) is a defined text-preservation measure based on the exact Levenshtein distance between generated and post-edited words.

100 × [1 − Σ word edits / Σ max(generated words, post-edited words)]

Each substitution, insertion or deletion costs one operation. Word tokenization is case-sensitive, uses Unicode NFC, excludes punctuation as separate tokens and keeps internal apostrophes and hyphens. The calculation uses the maximum length of each pair before summing.

Word edit rate (2.76%) divides the same 894 minimum word edits by 32,422 post-edited word tokens. Character edit rate (2.41%) compares characters after Unicode NFC and collapsed whitespace, normalized by the longer text in each pair.

Minimality describes preservation of wording. Translation quality is assessed separately in the human judgement distribution.

03TER against human post-edits+

Translation Edit Rate counts substitutions, insertions, deletions and phrase shifts needed to match a reference. Here the generated English text is compared with its reviewer post-edit: a human-targeted, or HTER-style, setting.

100 × 875 TER edits / 32,405 reference tokens = 2.70

The corpus score is computed with SacreBLEU 2.6.0 and one post-edited reference per text. It is case-insensitive, uses TERCOM tokenization, retains punctuation and disables additional normalization. A case-sensitive calculation gives 2.79.

The TER and word-edit calculations use their respective tokenization rules. The score is an editing-distance measure; 2.70 means 2.70 TER operations per 100 reference tokens.

04Highlights, coverage & edit localisation+

The study contains 390 highlight spans and 431 category assignments. Several categories can refer to the same span or text, so category counts overlap. All exported highlight offsets match the original English text.

Coverage is the union of highlighted word tokens, counting overlaps once: 925 / 32,461 = 2.85%. Character coverage is 3.05%. A highlighted passage may include surrounding context as well as the word being discussed.

Within the 339 post-edit pairs, 520 of 623 original word tokens replaced or deleted in a minimum-edit alignment fall inside highlights (83.47%). Conversely, 520 of 858 highlighted word tokens in this subset are replaced or deleted (60.61%). These describe where observed edits occurred; insertions are outside this comparison.

05Intervals & aggregation+

Corpus editing scores pool the edit counts and their denominators across texts. Percentile intervals use 10,000 bootstrap resamples with a fixed seed, grouping identical Greek source texts into 340 clusters. The intervals describe resampling uncertainty within this collection and its observed reviewer mix.
